Step 2 — The Levers, In Order

Harnish's order, plus Einstein's eighth

Each card shows a small nudge — usually 1% or 1 day — and what that nudge is worth this quarter. Adjust the nudge on any card to test bigger or smaller moves. Walked in the order Harnish lists them — Price through Inventory — with Truck Utilization appended as Einstein's eighth.

Why Inventory ≠ Truck Utilization

The classic Inventory lever is cash tied up in unsold goods. Einstein does carry packing-materials and supplies inventory, but it isn't tracked or valued today — so this lever runs at a placeholder $0 for lack of data, not because nothing's there. Real room may exist once it's actually counted; until then, treat Truck Utilization as the more useful eighth lever. Truck utilization is real money, and it isn't a cash-conversion issue — it's an overhead / fixed-asset efficiency issue: fewer, fuller trucks means less paid for insurance and maintenance on capacity you're not using.

So it gets its own lever

Truck Utilization runs as an eighth, Einstein-specific lever below — modeled the same way CashFlowStory models the standard seven, just built around what actually drives your cost structure: movers and trucks, not shelf inventory.

Step 5 — Run It Quarterly

How Harnish prescribes the workshop

Adapted from Scaling Up, p. 231 — run as a dedicated session each quarter, timed to Einstein's QGR close, not folded into a longer meeting.

1

Book a standalone Power of One session with the senior team — not a line item inside a longer meeting.

2

Work the eight levers one at a time, in order, using this page as the whiteboard.

3

Rank each lever — how much real room does the business have to move it this quarter?

4

Leave with 4–5 ideas the team will actually execute — not a long wish list.

5

Name an owner, an action, and a date for each idea chosen. No owner, no idea — use the Action Plan below.
